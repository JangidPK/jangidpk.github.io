# About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Chemical Physics at the Indian Institute of Science Education
 and Research (IISER) Pune, India. My research lies at the intersection of 
 **statistical mechanics, stochastic processes, and nonequilibrium dynamics**, with a 
 focus on understanding complex chemical and biological systems through physically
  interpretable, analytically grounded models — and, increasingly, on connecting that 
  theoretical foundation to data through machine learning and inference-driven methods.

During my Ph.D., I have developed analytical and computational models spanning
 **stochastic thermodynamics, non-Markovian dynamics, first-passage and transition-path theory, dynamic catalysis and reaction kinetics, molecular transport, and protein conformational and target-search dynamics**. My work combines theory, numerical simulations, and statistical mechanics to uncover the microscopic mechanisms governing far-from-equilibrium chemical and biological processes.

Alongside this theoretical core, I have built a parallel line of work using machine 
learning to extend and connect with mechanistic modeling — including physics-informed
 neural networks that learn memory kernels and transport coefficients directly from
  data, sequence models for characterizing anomalous diffusion, and generative models
   for molecular design. I see these data-driven tools not as a replacement for
    mechanistic understanding, but as a way to extract it from systems and datasets 
    where purely analytical approaches fall short — a perspective that extends 
    naturally to inference-driven and autonomous, data-guided experimentation.

My long-term goal is to build a research program that uses the combined tools of
 stochastic theory and machine learning to understand — and ultimately help discover
  and control — how order, transport, and function emerge in chemical and biological
   systems driven far from equilibrium.

## Research Philosophy

I enjoy working at the interface of **theory, computation, and chemistry**. My research philosophy centers on building models that are analytically grounded and physically interpretable, so that predictive power comes hand-in-hand with mechanistic insight. I bring the same standard to data-driven methods: I use them where they genuinely extend what mechanistic theory alone can reach — inferring hidden structure, kernels, or dynamics from data — rather than as black boxes that substitute for understanding.
